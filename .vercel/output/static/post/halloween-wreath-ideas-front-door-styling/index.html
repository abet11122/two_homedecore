A Halloween wreath is the first seasonal statement your home makes and the one that is visible to everyone who passes. The right wreath sets the tone for the whole exterior display. This guide covers 15 styles from classic to contemporary, with guidance on materials, installation, and matching the wreath to the house.

1. The Dark Botanical Wreath

A wreath made from dried black roses, dark eucalyptus, dried seed pods, and deep burgundy dried flowers creates a gothic botanical look that suits a neutral or dark exterior. This is the most sophisticated Halloween wreath style.

Make one yourself by wiring dried materials to a wire wreath frame, or buy from an Etsy seller who works in dried botanicals.

2. The Classic Black and Orange Wreath

A wreath combining black and orange elements — black faux leaves, orange pumpkins, and black ribbon — is the most recognisable Halloween wreath style. It suits a traditional house and reads clearly from the street.

Faux wreath kits in black and orange are available at Halloween shops and home stores in October.

3. The Dried Eucalyptus Wreath

A wreath made from dried eucalyptus in dark or natural tones adds a subtle seasonal note without any obvious Halloween imagery. It suits a minimalist or Scandinavian-style home.

Dried eucalyptus wreaths are available at florists and on Etsy. They last the whole season and can be used year-round with different ribbon colours.

4. The Pink-o-ween Wreath

A wreath in blush and black — pink faux flowers, black bats, and a pink ribbon — suits the Pink-o-ween trend and appeals to a Gen Z or Millennial-mom audience. It is one of the most photographed Halloween wreath styles of 2026.

Pink Halloween wreaths are available on Etsy and at some Halloween shops. Alternatively, add pink ribbon and pink faux flowers to a plain black wreath.

5. The Vintage Halloween Wreath

A wreath made from dried orange slices, cinnamon sticks, dried seed pods, and small gourds in a warm amber palette creates a vintage Halloween look. Add a vintage-style ribbon in cream or dark orange.

Dried orange slices are available at craft stores and online. They add a warm, spiced fragrance to the wreath.

6. The Feather and Skull Wreath

A wreath with black feathers and one or two small skull details creates a gothic, slightly theatrical look. Choose feathers with a natural, slightly dishevelled quality rather than perfectly arranged ones.

Feather wreaths are available at Halloween shops and on Etsy. They are one of the most dramatic wreath styles and suit a dark or Victorian-style exterior.

7. The Minimalist Black Wreath

A simple wreath made from black faux leaves or black dried materials with a single ribbon creates a minimalist Halloween look that suits a modern or contemporary home. The restraint is the point.

A minimalist wreath works best on a light-coloured door where the black reads clearly.

8. The Pumpkin Wreath

A wreath with small faux or ceramic pumpkins as the dominant element creates a playful, family-friendly Halloween look. Choose pumpkins in muted tones — cream, terracotta, and faded orange — rather than bright ones.

Pumpkin wreaths are available at home stores and on Etsy. They are one of the most popular Halloween wreath styles for families with young children.

9. The Spider Web Wreath

A wreath with a faux spider web stretched across the centre and a large spider as the focal point creates a classic Halloween look. Choose a web with a slightly aged, dusty quality rather than a bright white one.

Spider web wreaths are available at Halloween shops. They are one of the most recognisable Halloween wreath styles and suit a traditional or playful exterior.

10. The Dried Corn and Gourd Wreath

A wreath made from dried corn husks, small gourds, and autumn leaves creates a harvest-style Halloween wreath that suits a farmhouse or traditional exterior. It works from September through November.

Dried corn and gourd wreaths are available at farm shops and garden centres in autumn.

11. The DIY Twig Wreath

A wreath made from bare twigs or branches gathered from the garden creates a simple, slightly eerie Halloween look. Bind the twigs into a circle with wire and add one or two dark details — a ribbon, a skull, or a few dried flowers.

A twig wreath is free to make and creates a more natural, organic look than a foam or wire wreath.

12. The Velvet Ribbon Wreath

A plain wreath — dried eucalyptus, faux leaves, or a simple twig wreath — with a large velvet ribbon bow as the focal point creates an elegant, restrained Halloween look. Choose ribbon in deep plum, black, or dark burgundy.

The ribbon is the most important element of this wreath. Choose a wide ribbon (at least 6cm) and tie it in a full, generous bow.

13. The Glow-in-the-Dark Wreath

A wreath with UV-reactive or glow-in-the-dark elements — painted pumpkins, glow-in-the-dark spiders, or UV-reactive ribbon — creates a Halloween wreath that transforms after dark. Under normal light, it reads as a standard Halloween wreath. In the dark, it glows.

UV-reactive spray paint is available at craft stores. Apply it to any wreath element for a glow effect.

14. The Monogram Wreath

A wreath with a letter monogram at the centre — the first letter of the family name — adds a personalised detail to the Halloween display. Use a black or dark metal letter and surround it with dark seasonal materials.

Monogram letters are available at craft stores and home stores. They are available in a range of sizes and finishes.

15. Match the Wreath to the House

The most important rule for choosing a Halloween wreath is to match it to the style of the house. A gothic wreath on a minimalist house looks out of place. A minimalist black wreath on a traditional house looks underwhelming.

Choose a wreath that suits the exterior colour, the architectural style, and the interior decor. The wreath is the first impression — it should feel like it belongs.

Final Thoughts

A Halloween wreath is the highest-impact single change you can make to the exterior of your home. Choose a style that suits the house, use dried materials for durability, and hang it with an over-the-door hook for a renter-friendly installation. The wreath sets the tone for everything else.

Still wondering

Common questions

What makes a good Halloween wreath?

A good Halloween wreath has a clear visual identity — a specific colour palette, a dominant material, and one or two focal point elements. It should suit the style of the house and the interior decor. Dried materials are the most durable for outdoor use. A wreath that is too small for the door reads as an afterthought — choose one that fills at least two-thirds of the door width.

How do I hang a Halloween wreath without damaging the door?

Use an over-the-door wreath hanger — a metal hook that sits over the top of the door without nails or adhesive. They are available at hardware stores and home stores for a few dollars. For a lighter wreath, a removable adhesive hook on the door surface also works.

Most bathrooms are lit by one fitting in the middle of the ceiling, which is the worst possible position for the one task the room exists for. Light from directly above drops shadows into your eye sockets and under your nose and chin — exactly where you need to see.

Everything below follows from fixing that.

## First, the part that is not a styling decision

Bathrooms are wet environments, and fixed light fittings near a bath or shower have to be rated for their position. Those requirements come from national wiring regulations, and **they differ between countries** — the zone system and the ratings used in the UK are not the same as the rules in the US or elsewhere.

So: treat any fixed fitting, any new wiring, and anything that involves opening a wall as an electrician's job, and check what applies where you live before buying. A fitting sold online as "bathroom suitable" is not automatically compliant with your local rules or appropriate for the spot you want it.

Portable, battery-powered and plug-in options are a different matter, but they still belong well away from the bath and the shower.

## Light the face from the sides

This is the whole point of bathroom lighting, and it is a position problem rather than a brightness one.

**Two sources, one either side of the mirror, at roughly face height** — about 160–170 cm from the floor for most people. Light arrives across the face from both sides, and each source cancels the shadow the other would cast. This is why theatre dressing rooms are lit this way.

If sconces are not possible:

- **A strip or bar above the mirror** is a clear improvement on a ceiling fitting, though it still casts downward.
- **A backlit or edge-lit mirror** achieves something similar in one unit.
- **A single sconce on one side** is still better than overhead alone, especially combined with a pale opposite wall to bounce light back.

Keep the ceiling fitting. It is useful as general fill, ideally on a dimmer. It is just the wrong thing to rely on alone.

## The two bulb numbers

**Colour temperature: 2700–3000K.** Warm enough to be comfortable at six in the morning and at midnight. Above 4000K reads clinical, and makes white tile look grey and skin look ill.

**CRI 90 or above.** Colour rendering index is what decides whether skin tones, tile and towels look like themselves. Below 80, a bathroom looks flat and slightly dead regardless of how bright it is. This matters more in a bathroom than almost anywhere else, because you are looking at your own face.

Both are printed on the box. Neither costs much extra.

## Build it in layers

As in any room, several sources beat one bright one — [how to make a dark room brighter](/post/how-to-make-a-dark-room-brighter/) covers the principle in general.

**Task** — the mirror lights. The ones that matter.

**Ambient** — the ceiling fitting, dimmed, for general fill.

**Low or accent** — a small lamp on a shelf where there is a safe, dry spot for it, a lit niche in the shower, or a strip under a vanity. A low light left on is far kinder than the main light at night.

## In a bathroom with no window

A windowless bathroom lives entirely on its electric light, which makes the quality of it the single biggest decision in the room.

- Get the mirror lighting right first; it does more than anything else.
- Keep surfaces light and reflective so what light there is bounces — [small bathroom paint colors](/post/small-bathroom-paint-colors/) covers the finishes that help.
- A mirror on the wall beside, rather than opposite, the main light spreads it further.
- Resist the urge to simply fit a brighter bulb. Position beats brightness every time.

## What renters can change

The bulb, immediately. 2700–3000K at CRI 90+ in whatever fitting is already there costs almost nothing and fixes the colour cast, which is half the problem.

After that, a rechargeable LED strip stuck under a shelf or along the edge of a cabinet adds light at a better angle with no wiring and no holes — keeping it away from the bath and shower. ## Where it goes wrong

- **Relying on one ceiling fitting.** Wrong position for the only job that matters.
- **Cool white bulbs.** Clinical light, grey tile, bad mirror.
- **Low CRI.** Everything looks flat, including you.
- **Buying a fitting before checking local wiring rules.** They vary by country.
- **Brighter instead of better placed.** Position first.
- **A mirror light above only.** Sides beat top.

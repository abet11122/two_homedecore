Most rooms tagged Japandi online are beige minimalism with a paper lamp in them. The actual look comes from a specific combination, and understanding which half supplies what makes it much easier to get right.

## Two traditions, each supplying something

**From Japanese interiors:** restraint, low proportions, natural materials left visible, asymmetry, and an acceptance of wear and irregularity. Space treated as a material in its own right.

**From Scandinavian interiors:** warmth, soft textiles, pale wood, practical comfort, and light handled carefully because there is little of it for half the year.

Remove the Japanese half and you get a Scandi room. Remove the Scandinavian half and you get something austere and a bit cold. **Japandi is the overlap, and it fails when it tips too far either way.**

## The palette

A narrow range of warm neutrals — oatmeal, bone, clay, putty, mushroom — **grounded by something much darker**. Charcoal, black, deep walnut brown.

That dark element is the single most-missed part. Without it the scheme drifts into undifferentiated beige, which is exactly what most of the online examples are. The dark does not need much area: a black-framed picture, a charcoal cushion, a dark wooden stool.

One muted natural accent is usually enough. An earthy green, a rust, a faded indigo.

## Proportions: low, and off the floor

Two things do more for the look than any object you could buy.

**Low furniture.** A low-backed sofa, a low coffee table, a bench rather than a tall sideboard. Lowering the furniture line raises the apparent ceiling and leaves a band of calm wall above everything.

**Visible floor.** Pieces on legs, with space underneath. Floor you can see reads as space — the same reason it works in [why your living room feels empty](/post/living-room-feels-empty/), applied here for calm rather than volume.

A rug matters, but it should sit within the room rather than reaching the walls. [What size rug do you need](/post/what-size-rug-do-i-need/) has the margins.

## Materials, and what to avoid

The look is carried almost entirely by material rather than by shape.

**Use:** solid wood with visible grain, linen, wool, paper, rattan, unglazed or matte ceramic, stone, raw cotton, bamboo.

**Avoid:** high-gloss anything, chrome, printed woodgrain, plastic, heavy pattern, and the synthetic velvet that has been everywhere for several years.

Wood tone matters more here than in most schemes, because there is usually a lot of it and little else competing. Two or three tones that share an undertone, with one dominant — [how to mix wood tones](/post/how-to-mix-wood-tones/) covers the method and the near-match trap.

## Texture instead of pattern

A Japandi room is almost entirely plain, which means texture has to do the work pattern would normally do. Slubby linen, a chunky wool throw, a ribbed ceramic vase, a paper shade, an open-weave basket.

Get the texture wrong and the room is flat and cold. This is the difference between Japandi and plain minimalism, and it is where [minimalist living rooms](/post/minimalist-living-room-guide/) and this look part company.

## Light it softly

Diffused and low. Paper or linen shades, several small sources rather than one bright one, warm 2700K bulbs with good colour rendering so the wood and linen read as themselves. Nothing bare, nothing cold, no downlights glaring onto a pale floor.

## Leave the imperfection in

This is the part most often sanded out, and it is a genuine principle rather than a styling note.

A hand-thrown bowl with an uneven rim. A wooden board with a crack in it. A linen cushion that creases. Furniture with visible joints. A room where everything matches perfectly and nothing shows a mark reads as a showroom, which is the opposite of what the style is reaching for.

Asymmetry too: group objects in threes at different heights rather than arranging them symmetrically.

## Where it goes wrong

- **All beige.** Add something genuinely dark.
- **Tall, bulky furniture.** Low and on legs.
- **Gloss, chrome or printed grain.** Material is the whole thing.
- **Plain without texture.** That is minimalism, and it reads cold.
- **Everything matching.** Imperfection and asymmetry are part of it.
- **Bright overhead light.** Diffused, warm, several sources.

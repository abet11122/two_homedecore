Scale, walkways, rug size, closed storage. A small living room is decided by those four things, and only one of them is really about what the furniture looks like. Keep walkways 75–90 cm wide, get a rug big enough to anchor the seating, and shut the clutter away. Measure first, choose one of a handful of proven layouts, and light the room at three heights.

These ideas suit living rooms of roughly 9–15 m² (100–160 sq ft) — common in one-bedroom apartments.

## Measure Before You Move Anything

Sketch the room on graph paper (1 square = 10 cm) and mark:

- Doors and their swing
- Windows and radiators
- Sockets, the TV aerial or cable point, and light switches
- The main path from the door to other rooms

Then measure any furniture you're keeping. This takes 20 minutes and saves buying a sofa that doesn't fit.

## Choose Furniture to Scale

| Piece | Small-room size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sofa | 150–200 cm, seat depth 85–95 cm | Slim arms, visible legs |
| Armchair | 65–80 cm wide | One chair beats a second sofa |
| Coffee table | About two-thirds the sofa length | Round or oval is easier to walk around |
| Media unit | 120–160 cm wide, 35–45 cm deep | Doors hide clutter |
| Side table | 35–45 cm across | C-shaped tables slide over the sofa arm |
| Rug | 140 × 200 cm or 160 × 230 cm | At least the front legs of all seating on it |

Slim arms make a big difference: a sofa with 25 cm arms on each side is 50 cm longer than its seats.

## Four Layouts That Work

**1. Sofa facing the TV.** Sofa on the longest wall, TV on the opposite wall, coffee table between them, one armchair angled at the side. The classic layout for a rectangular room.

**2. Floating sofa.** Pull the sofa off the wall to create a walkway behind it, or to divide the living area from a dining table. Needs at least 75 cm behind the sofa.

**3. Corner L-shape.** A small corner sofa or a sofa plus a chair at right angles, tucked into a corner, opens up the center of the room. Works well when the door is in the middle of a long wall.

**4. Two chairs and a loveseat.** A loveseat of 130–150 cm plus two small chairs around a round coffee table. Good for conversation and flexible for odd-shaped rooms.

## Keep the Clearances

- **Walkways:** 75–90 cm.
- **Sofa to coffee table:** 40–45 cm.
- **Sofa to TV:** about 1.5–2.5 times the screen's diagonal.
- **Around a radiator:** don't push the sofa against it; leave 10–15 cm so heat can circulate.

## Rugs

### Choose the right size

A rug that only fits under the coffee table makes a room look smaller. In a small living room, go for at least 140 × 200 cm, so the front legs of every seat sit on it.

### Choose the right material

Flatweave and low-pile rugs are easier to clean and don't catch on doors. Wool is warm and durable; washable polyester rugs suit homes with pets and children.

## Storage

- **Media unit with doors:** hides consoles, cables and remotes.
- **Storage ottoman:** a coffee table, footrest and blanket box in one.
- **Tall, narrow bookcase:** 180–200 cm high and 40–60 cm wide. Anchor it to the wall.
- **Floating shelves:** above the sofa (20–30 cm above the back) or around the TV.
- **Baskets under a console:** throws, games and magazines.

For more space-finding ideas, see [small apartment organization ideas](/post/small-apartment-organization-ideas/).

## Lighting at Three Heights

1. **Overhead:** a simple fitting for cleaning and daytime.
2. **Mid-height:** a table lamp on a side table or console.
3. **Floor lamp:** beside the sofa or in a corner, for reading and evening light.

Warm-white 2700K bulbs. Turn off the overhead in the evening and the room immediately feels larger and calmer.

## Make It Feel Bigger

- Hang curtains near the ceiling and past the window frame.
- Use one large piece of art rather than many small frames.
- Add a large mirror opposite or beside the window.
- Choose a consistent palette of three colors.
- Keep the coffee table and console mostly clear.

## Layout Errors
- **A sofa that's too deep.** Over 100 cm deep eats the room.
- **A tiny rug.** Size up.
- **Furniture against every wall.** Pull at least one piece in.
- **Blocking the radiator.** Leave space for heat to circulate.
- **Only one ceiling light.**

## Price Guide

| Item | Typical cost |
| --- | --- |
| Compact sofa (150–200 cm) | $400–$1,200 |
| Armchair | $150–$500 |
| Rug, 160 × 230 cm | $100–$350 |
| Media unit with doors | $120–$400 |
| Storage ottoman | $40–$120 |
| Floor lamp and table lamp | $60–$200 |

The sofa and armchair take most of the budget. Put any savings into a rug that is big enough.

The mantel is the one surface in a living room that everybody looks at in December, which is why it gets overloaded. Three heights, one garland cut long enough to drape, and about a third of the shelf left showing is the whole method — the same one that works for [the autumn mantel](/post/autumn-mantel-styling/), with different materials.

Before any of it, though, there is a safety step that seasonal decorating guides tend to skip.

## Check what the fireplace needs

If the fireplace works, the shelf above it sits in a heat path. Dried foliage, paper, ribbon and fabric are all combustible, and a mantel that feels cool in July can get genuinely warm with a fire lit beneath it.

Clearances vary by appliance and are specified by the manufacturer — they are in the manual, and they are the number to work to rather than a rule of thumb from the internet. Where you cannot find them or cannot be confident, the safe version is simple: decorate an unused fireplace, and keep a working one's shelf bare apart from non-combustible pieces well back from the edge.

The same applies to stockings. Anything hanging below the shelf is closer to the fire than the things on top of it.

## Clear it completely first

Take everything off, including whatever lives there year-round. Decorating around the existing clock and photographs is why most mantels end up crowded — you are adding to a full shelf rather than composing one.

## Get the garland length right

This is the measurement people get wrong, and it is the difference between a garland that drapes and one that looks like it is being stretched to fit.

Aim for **about one and a half times the shelf length**. A 120 cm mantel wants roughly 180 cm of garland; a 150 cm mantel wants around 225 cm. The surplus gives you the dip at the centre and the tails falling at each end.

Fix it with removable hooks under the lip of the shelf rather than on the face, so nothing shows when it comes down. On a stone or brick surround, adhesive fixings often fail — check what the surface is, and test one hook somewhere hidden before trusting the whole garland to them.

If a full garland is too much for the room, a single swag off one end reads as deliberate and uses half the material.

## Build in three heights

The method that works year-round, applied to December:

**Tall — the anchor.** One item, roughly two-thirds the height of whatever hangs above the mantel. A pair of tall candlesticks, a single branch in a heavy vase, a narrow lantern. Set it off-centre.

**Medium — the mass.** Two or three pieces at about half the anchor's height, grouped rather than spaced evenly. Small lanterns, a cluster of bottle-brush trees, a bowl of baubles.

**Low — the detail.** Things that run along the shelf and fill the gaps at the base. Foliage, pine cones, a string of small lights, a few matte baubles resting directly on the wood.

Then stop. **Leave about a third of the shelf visible.** Empty shelf is what makes the full parts read as styled rather than stacked.

## Light it properly

A mantel is usually at eye level in a dark corner of the room in December, which makes it the single best place in a living room for warm light.

- **Candles** in varied heights, if the clearances allow it. LED tapers where they do not, or where the room has children or pets.
- **A string of warm white lights** worked through the garland. Battery-powered avoids a cable down the chimney breast.
- **2700K bulbs**, not cool white. Cold light makes greenery look grey and metallics look cheap — [how to make a dark room brighter](/post/how-to-make-a-dark-room-brighter/) covers why.

## Keep the palette tight

Two or three colours across the whole shelf. Green and cream with brass. Red and green if you want the classic, but pick one to dominate. White and silver for something colder.

The mistake is treating a mantel as the place to display every decoration you own. A tight palette is what separates a mantel that looks composed from one that looks like storage.

## What goes wrong

- **Decorating a working fireplace without checking its clearances.** The manual, not a rule of thumb.
- **A garland cut to the exact shelf length.** It needs half as much again to drape.
- **Filling the whole shelf.** Leave a third showing.
- **Everything at one height.** Three levels, or it reads flat.
- **Cool white lights.** They turn greenery grey.
- **Adhesive hooks on stone or brick.** Check the surface first.

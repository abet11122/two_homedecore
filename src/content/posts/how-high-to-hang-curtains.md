Most curtains in most homes are hung too low, too narrow, and too short. The window ends up looking smaller than it is, and the room reads as unfinished even when everything else in it is right. Three measurements fix that, and none of them cost anything.

## Height: above the frame, not on it

Brackets screwed to the top of the window frame put the curtain exactly where the window stops. The eye reads the whole arrangement as one short block.

Fix the rod **10–15 cm (4–6 in) above the frame** and the fabric starts above the glass. The window looks taller because nothing marks where it ends.

Where the gap between the frame and the ceiling is small — under about 30 cm — splitting that gap looks worse, not better, because the band of wall above the rod becomes a visible stripe. Go the other way instead: fix the rod **5–10 cm below the ceiling**, or use a ceiling-mounted track, and let the curtain run the full height of the wall.

One exception worth knowing: if the room has a picture rail, coving or a deep cornice, hang just below it rather than cutting across it.

## Width: past the frame on both sides

An open curtain has to sit somewhere. If the rod stops at the frame, it sits on the glass, and you lose both the daylight and the look of the full window.

Run the rod **15–25 cm (6–10 in) past the frame on each side**. The open panels stack onto wall instead of window. In a dark room this is the single cheapest way to get more light — see [how to make a dark room brighter](/post/how-to-make-a-dark-room-brighter/) for the rest of it.

Two constraints override the rule. A window close to a corner takes whatever wall is there. And a rod longer than about 150 cm needs a centre support bracket, which means the curtains cannot draw all the way across in one pass unless you use a track or a bracket the rings pass over.

## Width again: how much fabric

This is the measurement people skip, and it is the one that decides whether the result looks like curtains or like a bedsheet.

Add the flat width of both panels together. You want **2 to 2.5 times the width of the rod**. A 180 cm rod wants roughly 360–450 cm of curtain in total, so two panels of about 180–225 cm each.

Under 1.5 times, the fabric has no fullness and hangs flat when closed. Sheers can take 2.5–3 times, because the gathers are the point.

## Drop: measure from the rod

Measure from the top of the rod — or from the eye of the ring the hook goes into, which is what actually carries the curtain — down to the floor. Not from the frame. People measure from the frame, order to that number, and end up with curtains that stop 15 cm short.

Then decide the finish before you order:

| Finish | Gap at the floor | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floating | About 1 cm clear | Everyday rooms, radiators under the window, anywhere that gets vacuumed often |
| Kissing | Touching, no fold | A tailored look, if your floor is level |
| Breaking | 2–5 cm of fabric resting on the floor | Heavy linen, velvet, a softer room |
| Puddling | 10 cm or more | Bedrooms and formal rooms; not where a door, pet or walkway crosses |

Ready-made curtains come in fixed drops — commonly 137, 183, 229 and 274 cm (54, 72, 90 and 108 in). Measure first, then buy the next length up and hem, rather than the length down and hope.

## Fitting the brackets

Mark the rod height first, then use a spirit level across to the second bracket — windows and ceilings are often not level, and a curtain follows the rod, which makes any error obvious.

Check what the wall is made of before you drill. Plasterboard needs the right anchor for the load; a curtain plus a lined panel on a long rod is heavier than people expect, and a bracket pulling out takes a chunk of wall with it. The [floating shelf checklist](/post/diy-floating-shelves/) covers how to identify a wall type and choose a fixing, and the same checks apply here.

## Where it usually goes wrong

- **Brackets on the frame itself.** The commonest mistake, and the one with the biggest effect.
- **A rod the same width as the window.** The curtains eat the glass every morning.
- **Two narrow panels.** Fullness is fabric width, not rod length.
- **Measuring the drop from the frame.** Measure from the rod.
- **A rod that is not level.** Use a level, not the top of the window.
- **Hems above the floor.** It shortens the whole wall.

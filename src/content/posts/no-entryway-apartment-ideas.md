This is a different problem from a small entryway. A small entryway is a space to organise. No entryway at all means the front door opens directly into the living room, and there is nothing to organise — you have to make the space first.

The good news is that it takes less room than people expect: roughly **one square metre**.

## Draw the boundary first

Without a defined edge, coats, bags and shoes spread into the room, because nothing tells them where to stop. The boundary does more work than any piece of furniture.

**A rug or a large doormat.** The simplest and most effective move, and usually the only one available in a rental. Pick something clearly different in colour or texture from the main floor. A flat weave takes wet shoes and sits under the door.

**A change of flooring**, if you own the place. Tile or a different board direction in the first metre. Permanent, and the most convincing version.

**A piece of furniture as a wall.** A bookcase or a tall shelf unit standing perpendicular to the wall, forming one side of the zone. [Zoning a studio](/post/studio-apartment-zoning/) covers using furniture this way across a whole room.

**A screen.** A folding screen beside the door hides the drop zone from the sofa and takes almost no floor when folded.

## Then build upward

Floor space is what you do not have, so the entry lives on the wall.

**Hooks at 165–175 cm.** One per person, plus one spare. 100–120 cm for children. This is the single most useful fitting.

**A shelf of 20 cm at 85–95 cm high.** Keys, post, the thing you must not forget tomorrow. At console height it reads as furniture rather than as a shelf.

**A mirror.** Does the last-look job and bounces light into what is often the darkest corner of the room. On the wall beside the door, not facing it.

**A shoe tray underneath.** Defined, wipeable, and it keeps wet shoes off the rug. [Shoe storage for a small entryway](/post/shoe-storage-small-entryway/) covers the depths if you want something more than a tray.

**A small basket on the shelf** for keys, cards and the pile of small things that otherwise migrate to the kitchen counter.

## Use what is already there

In a room with no hall, the furniture you already own can take the job.

**The back of the sofa.** If the sofa sits near the door, its back can form the entry's boundary. A narrow console behind it — 20–25 cm — becomes the drop surface, which is a genuinely elegant solution because it needs no new floor at all.

**The side of a wardrobe or bookcase.** Hooks on the side facing the door rather than the room.

**The wall behind the door swing.** Dead space in most flats. Hooks here are invisible when the door is open, which is exactly when a guest is looking.

**Inside the nearest cupboard door.** An over-door rack for shoes or a hook rail, hidden completely.

## Point it away from the room

The thing that makes an in-room entry look like a mess is that you see it from the sofa.

So face the storage **towards the door and away from the seating**. Hooks behind the door swing, the console back to the room, the screen between the two. A guest sitting down should see the back of a bookcase, not four coats and a bag of recycling.

Where that is impossible, reduce instead: one coat per person at the door, the rest in a bedroom. A tidy row of three coats reads very differently from nine.

## Protect the floor

The first metre of a flat with no hall takes everything the street brings in, and in a rental the flooring is not yours.

- A doormat **outside** the door as well as in, if the building allows it.
- A tray for wet shoes, always.
- A washable rug rather than a precious one.
- Felt pads under anything that stands there, because that square metre gets knocked constantly.

## Where it goes wrong

- **No boundary.** Without a rug or an edge, the entry spreads into the room.
- **Trying to fit a hall's worth of furniture.** One square metre, mostly vertical.
- **Storage facing the sofa.** Turn it towards the door.
- **Hooks on the wrong wall.** Behind the door swing is usually best.
- **Too many coats at the door.** One per person; the rest live elsewhere.
- **A precious rug in the wettest square metre of the flat.** Washable, flat weave.

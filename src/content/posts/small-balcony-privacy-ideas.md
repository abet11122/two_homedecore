Two things decide what you can do here, and neither is aesthetic: **what the building allows**, and **how much wind the balcony gets**. Work those out and the choice narrows itself.

## Read the rules first

Many leases and building regulations restrict what can be attached to a railing or an exterior wall. The reasons are usually structural, visual uniformity, or fire safety — and in a lot of buildings barbecues and certain materials are prohibited outright.

Check before buying. Where attaching is allowed, remember that anything fixed to a railing increases the load on it in high wind, which is exactly when you would least like to find out it was not rated for that.

Renters: get it in writing, and photograph the railing and the wall before anything goes on.

## Understand the wind

A balcony is the most exposed part of the home, and a **solid screen is a sail**. In a gust it does not deflect the wind, it catches it, and the force goes into whatever is holding it.

This is why so many balcony privacy projects end up on the floor, or worse, on the pavement below.

**Permeable screening — slatted, mesh, woven willow — lets air through.** It is less visually solid, and it survives. On an exposed balcony, that is the whole trade and it is not close.

Whatever you fit, check the fixings at the start of each season.

## Screen the right direction, at the right height

Most balconies do not need screening on all sides. Stand on yours and work out where you are actually overlooked from — usually one neighbour, or the flats opposite.

Then sit down. **Privacy at seated eye level is what matters**, because that is how a balcony gets used. Screening to standing height takes more material, more wind load and more light, for a benefit you get for about four seconds at a time.

A screen to roughly 120–150 cm usually does it.

## The options

**Reed, willow or bamboo screening.** Rolls that zip-tie to a railing. Cheap, permeable, quick, and it softens the look of metal railings considerably. Expect two to four seasons before it goes brittle. Use UV-stable ties and plenty of them.

**Privacy mesh or balcony netting.** Purpose-made fabric panels with eyelets. Permeable, long-lasting, and they come in sizes designed for standard railings. Less charming than willow, more durable.

**Tall planters.** A row of deep pots with tall grasses, bamboo or small evergreens. The softest-looking option and the most work — see [balcony container garden ideas](/post/balcony-container-garden-ideas/) for the weight and watering realities. Needs nothing attached to the building, which makes it the renter's answer.

**A freestanding screen.** A folding or slatted screen weighted at the base. No fixings, moves for cleaning, comes with you. Needs genuine weight at the bottom, and it should come in when a storm is forecast.

**Outdoor curtains.** On a tension wire or a rod between two fixed points. Lovely in still weather, and they need tying back the rest of the time. Best where you already have two solid uprights to work between.

**A parasol or a sail shade.** Deals with overlooking from above, which screening cannot. Must be fully retractable — a sail left up in a gale is the single most likely thing to end up on a neighbour's car.

## Which to choose

| Situation | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Renting, nothing can be attached | Tall planters, or a weighted freestanding screen |
| Exposed and windy | Permeable mesh or slatted screening, well fixed |
| Overlooked from one side only | Screening on that side at seated height |
| Overlooked from above | A sail shade or a parasol, retractable |
| Sheltered, and you want it to feel like a room | Outdoor curtains and planting |

## Keeping it usable

Privacy is only half of what makes a balcony somewhere you sit. The rest is the floor, the furniture and the light — deck tiles over tired concrete, a rail-mounted table, something warm at dusk. [Balcony decor ideas for small spaces](/post/balcony-decor-ideas/) covers the layout side, including keeping 24 inches clear to the door. At ground level the problem is different — [small patio ideas](/post/small-patio-ideas/) deals with courtyards and overlooked boundaries.

And keep the drainage in mind. Anything that blocks the gaps at the base of a railing can stop water draining, which ends up either pooling on your floor or running onto the balcony below.

## Where it goes wrong

- **A solid panel on an exposed balcony.** It is a sail. Use something permeable.
- **Fixing to the railing without checking the lease.** Many prohibit it.
- **Screening to standing height.** Seated eye level is what you need.
- **Lightweight pots as a windbreak.** They go over, and they go over loudly.
- **Blocking the drainage gaps.** The water has to go somewhere.
- **A sail shade left up.** Take it down before the weather arrives.

Good small bedroom storage reduces what ends up on the floor, the bed and the chair at the end of the day. Start with what needs a home, measure the room's moving parts — bed access, wardrobe doors, drawers — and only then choose storage. A large unit in the wrong spot can make the room harder to use than no unit at all.

This guide assumes a typical small bedroom of roughly 2.7 × 3 m (9 × 10 ft) with a double bed, but the measurements apply to any size.

## Step 1: Sort by How Often You Use Things

Pull out one category at a time and sort it into three groups:

- **Daily** — this week's clothes, phone charger, glasses, book.
- **Occasional** — going-out clothes, spare bedding, gym bag.
- **Seasonal or rare** — winter coats in summer, holiday decorations, suitcases.

Get rid of what you don't need before buying anything. Then note the size of what remains: a stack of jumpers needs a 30 cm deep shelf, a spare duvet needs a 60 × 40 × 20 cm bag, a pair of boots needs 40 cm of height.

Daily things go at waist-to-shoulder height near where you use them. Seasonal things can go under the bed, on top of the wardrobe or in an ottoman bed.

## Step 2: Map the Room's Moving Parts

| Clearance | Guideline | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beside the bed | About 60 cm (24 in); 45 cm minimum on one side | Walking past and making the bed |
| In front of a wardrobe or drawers | 75–90 cm (30–36 in) | Doors swing and drawers pull out while you stand there |
| Foot of the bed to a wall or furniture | About 60–75 cm | Walking route |
| Door swing | Full arc clear | The bedroom door must open fully |

Open every door and drawer in the room and tape their swing on the floor. Mark sockets, radiators and vents you can't cover. Then tape out the footprint of any new piece and walk around it — ideally with two people, if you share the room.

## Step 3: Get Under-Bed Storage Right

Under the bed is the largest hidden storage space in most bedrooms, but it's often measured wrong.

1. **Find the lowest point.** Measure from the floor to the underside of the frame at several spots, including the side rails and any central support. Many bed frames have only 15–18 cm.
2. **Measure the usable width and depth** between legs and supports.
3. **Measure the pull-out space.** A 60 cm deep bin needs 60 cm of clear floor beside the bed to come all the way out.

**Example:** the lowest point is 15 cm. A 12 cm-high bin leaves 3 cm, which carpet, wheels and handles can easily use up. A 10 cm bin will slide in reliably. Test with a cardboard box before buying a set.

| Option | Height | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wheeled under-bed drawer | 10–18 cm | Weekly-use bedding, shoes |
| Zip fabric bag | 15–20 cm | Off-season clothes |
| Vacuum bag | Shrinks to 5–10 cm | Duvets and pillows |
| Bed risers | Add 8–15 cm | Creating space on a low frame |
| Ottoman (lift-up) bed | 25–35 cm of storage | Suitcases, bulky bedding |

If you need a new bed anyway, a drawer bed or ottoman bed often replaces a chest of drawers entirely.

## Step 4: Fix the Wardrobe Before Adding Furniture

Most wardrobes waste space. Before buying new storage, look at what's wrong with the one you have:

- **Too much hanging, not enough shelving?** Add a hanging shelf organizer or stacked shelf dividers.
- **Short clothes on a full-length rail?** A second rail below doubles hanging space for shirts and skirts — you need about 100 cm of drop for each rail.
- **Things lost at the back of deep shelves?** Use open-fronted bins you can pull out like drawers.
- **Floor of the wardrobe a mess?** Two-tier shoe racks or a set of drawers fit there.
- **Space above the top shelf?** Lidded boxes for seasonal items.

Use slim, matching hangers. They can free 20–30% more rail space than chunky plastic or wooden ones.

## Step 5: Use the Bed Wall and Bedside

- **Headboard shelf.** A 10–15 cm deep shelf above or behind the headboard replaces a bedside table. Keep only light things on it — a book, a phone, glasses — and nothing that could fall on your head.
- **Wall-mounted bedside shelf or floating drawer.** Frees floor space in a tight room. A 30 × 25 cm surface is enough for a lamp and a glass of water.
- **Storage headboard.** Some headboards include cubbies or shelves, about 20–25 cm deep. Remember that it pushes the bed forward by that amount.
- **Wall lights or plug-in sconces** free the whole bedside surface.

## Step 6: Use Height Safely

A tall, narrow wardrobe or bookcase uses vertical space while keeping the floor clear. Put light, rarely used items up high and heavy, daily items low. Every tall unit must be anchored to the wall with the kit supplied — chests of drawers and wardrobes can tip forward when drawers are open.

Floating shelves above a desk or door add display and storage space. See the [floating shelf checklist](/post/diy-floating-shelves/) for wall types and fixings.

## Step 7: Use the Back of the Door

Over-door hooks hold a robe and tomorrow's outfit. A slim over-door organizer with pockets can hold accessories, scarves or shoes. Check that it doesn't stop the door closing, and add felt pads to stop rattling.

## Measuring Mistakes
- **Measuring under the bed at only one point.** The lowest point decides what fits.
- **A chest of drawers you can't open.** Leave 75–90 cm in front.
- **Not anchoring tall furniture.** Especially in children's rooms.
- **Buying storage before decluttering.** You'll organize things you don't need.
- **Blocking a radiator or vent.** It makes the room colder and can damage furniture.

## Run a One-Week Reset

Each night for a week, notice what lands on the bed, floor or chair. Tomorrow's outfit needs a hook. Laundry needs a hamper where clothes come off. Books and devices need a bedside spot. Fix the item that keeps returning first.

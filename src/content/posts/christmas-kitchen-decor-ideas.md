A kitchen counter is working space in December more than in any other month, so Christmas decor belongs above and around it rather than on it: windows, cabinet doors, open shelves and the space above the cabinets. Keep anything flammable away from the hob and oven, use decorations you can wipe clean, and let food — clementines, cinnamon, gingerbread — do some of the decorating.

The kitchen is a working room in December — probably the busiest of the year — so every idea here leaves the prep area clear.

## Rules for a Working Kitchen

1. **Keep the main prep counter clear.** No trays, no figurines.
2. **Keep the hob and oven zone free** of greenery, ribbon, paper and fabric. Heat rises — don't decorate above the cooker.
3. **Choose wipeable decorations.** Kitchens get greasy and steamy. Glass, metal, ceramic and faux greenery clean easily; felt and paper don't.
4. **Use LED lights,** which stay cool, and keep plugs away from the sink.

## Windows

### 1. Put lights in the window

A string of warm-white LEDs along the window frame, fixed with clear adhesive clips, is visible from inside and outside. Battery lights with a timer avoid a cable running across the counter.

### 2. Hang a small wreath in the window

Tie ribbon to the top of the window frame or a curtain rod and hang a 25–35 cm wreath in the center of the glass. Faux or dried wreaths last best near steam.

### 3. Add a garland on a tension rod

A tension rod across the top of the window holds a light garland with no screws. Keep it clear of a window you open often.

## Cabinets

### 4. Hang wreaths on cabinet doors

Small wreaths (20–30 cm) on two or three upper cabinet doors look festive at eye level. Loop ribbon over the top of the door and secure it inside with a small removable hook. Check the door still closes without catching.

### 5. Run garland along the top of upper cabinets

The space above upper cabinets suits a garland and lights well — out of the way and visible from across the room. Keep it to the cabinets away from the cooker. Faux garland is easier to dust off in January.

### 6. Tie ribbon on handles

Velvet ribbon bows on a few cabinet handles or the oven door handle are cheap and come off in seconds. Keep them off the oven itself if the handle gets hot.

## Shelves

### 7. Style one open shelf

If you have open shelves, decorate one. Swap a few everyday items for festive ones: a small faux tree, a stack of red or white plates, a jar of candy canes, a string of lights. See [open shelving styling](/post/kitchen-open-shelving-styling/) for the grouping and height rules.

### 8. Use a mug rail for ornaments

If you have a rail with hooks, hang a few shatterproof ornaments or mini stockings alongside the mugs.

## A Little on the Counter

### 9. One festive tray or bowl

Allow one decorative spot on the counter, away from the prep area. A bowl of clementines with their leaves, a tray with a candle and some pinecones, or a jar of cinnamon sticks.

### 10. A tabletop tree on the dining or breakfast table

A small tree (30–60 cm) on the breakfast bar or dining table adds height without taking prep space. Decorate it with dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks to match the kitchen.

### 11. Use the coffee or tea station

If you have a coffee corner, give it a festive touch: a holiday mug or two, a jar of peppermint sticks or a small sign.

## Textiles

### 12. Swap the tea towels

Festive tea towels on the oven handle or a hook are the cheapest kitchen decoration there is. Keep them away from the hob.

### 13. Add a table runner

A plaid or linen runner on the dining table, with a low centerpiece of greenery and candles. Keep centerpieces low so people can see each other across the table.

### 14. Put down a festive mat

A washable rug or mat by the sink in a holiday color or pattern adds warmth underfoot.

## Edible and Natural Decor

### 15. Decorate with food

- A bowl of clementines with leaves attached.
- Dried orange slices on twine — bake at about 90–100°C for 3–4 hours.
- A gingerbread house on a cake stand.
- A jar of candy canes or nuts in their shells.

They look festive, smell good and get eaten.

### 16. Use fresh herbs and greenery

A pot of rosemary trimmed into a little tree shape, or eucalyptus and fir sprigs in a jar of water. Keep them away from the hob.

### 17. Simmer a festive scent

A small pan of water with orange peel, cinnamon sticks, cloves and a little rosemary on a low heat makes the whole home smell like Christmas. Never leave it unattended, and top up the water so the pan doesn't boil dry.

## Kitchen Hazards to Watch
- **Decorating the prep counter.** Keep it clear for cooking.
- **Garland above the cooker.** Heat and steam rise.
- **Felt and paper decorations near the sink.** They won't survive.
- **Real candles near tea towels or paper.** Use LED candles.
- **Cables trailing across the counter.** Use battery lights.

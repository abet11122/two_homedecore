In December the entry becomes the busiest few square meters of the home, which makes decorating it mostly a question of what you leave clear. Give it a well-sized wreath on the door, one decorated surface inside, warm lights, and somewhere for guests' coats, boots and bags — then keep the walkway itself empty.

These ideas suit a small hall, a shared-building front door, or a studio where the "entry" is a strip of wall.

## The Front Door

### 1. Choose the right wreath size

| Door | Wreath size |
| --- | --- |
| Standard single door (about 90 cm / 36 in) | 50–60 cm (20–24 in) |
| Narrow door (75–80 cm) | 40–50 cm |
| Double doors | Two matching wreaths, 45–55 cm each |
| Glass panel door | Hang on ribbon so it's centered on the glass |

A wreath that's too small looks lost from the street. Aim for a third to half the door's width.

### 2. Hang it without damage

- **Over-door hanger:** check it suits your door's thickness and doesn't stop the door closing.
- **Magnetic hanger:** for metal doors.
- **Ribbon:** loop wide ribbon over the top of the door, and fix it inside with a removable hook.

Add felt pads to the back so it doesn't scratch the paint as the door moves.

### 3. Add a festive doormat — or layer two

A plain coir mat on top of a larger plaid or striped outdoor rug looks smart and catches more grit and snow. Check the door still clears both.

### 4. Frame the door (if you have space)

A garland around the door frame, held with clear adhesive hooks, makes a big impact. Keep it clear of the hinges and lock. Planters with small fir trees or winter greenery on each side of the door work on a wide step, but don't block a shared hallway or fire route.

## Inside the Entry

### 5. Choose one surface

Pick a console, a shelf, a radiator cover or the top of a shoe cabinet. Add a short garland, a small lamp or string of lights, and one or two objects: a lantern, a small tree, a bowl of pinecones. Keep the rest of the entry clear.

### 6. Style the console with three heights

A tall piece (a small tree or branches in a vase), a medium one (a lantern or candlesticks), and a low one (a bowl for keys, now filled with ornaments or clementines). See [entryway table decor ideas](/post/entryway-table-decor-ideas/) for the full formula.

### 7. Hang a garland on the stairs

If your entry has stairs, tie a garland along the outer side of the banister with ribbon or soft ties every 30–50 cm. Keep the handrail free to hold, and make sure no cords or ribbon trail onto the steps.

### 8. Use the mirror

A mirror in the entry looks festive with a small wreath hung over it on ribbon, or a sprig of greenery tucked into the corner of the frame.

### 9. Add a small tree in a basket

A tabletop tree (45–75 cm) in a woven basket on the floor, beside the console or in a corner, adds height without taking much space. Keep it out of the walkway.

## Light the Way

### 10. Warm lights inside

A string of warm-white LEDs through the garland, a battery lantern on the console, or a small lamp. A lit entry is welcoming when guests arrive after dark.

### 11. Outdoor-rated lights outside

Use lights labeled for outdoor use (IP44 or higher) and plug them into an outdoor socket or a weatherproof connector. Solar or battery lights avoid cables across the step. Put them on a timer.

### 12. Light the path

Solar stake lights or lanterns along a short path or on steps help guests find the door and avoid slips. Keep them clear of where people walk.

## Plan for Guests

### 13. Add temporary hooks

Guests bring coats. Add a row of over-door hooks or removable hooks for December, or clear the coat rack of your own off-season coats.

### 14. Put out a boot tray

A boot tray (around 35 × 75 cm) by the door catches snow and mud. A basket of spare slippers or socks is a nice touch for guests who take shoes off.

### 15. Make space for bags and gifts

Clear one shelf, bench or the bottom of a cupboard for guests' bags and gifts arriving. A basket works well.

## Finishing Touches

### 16. Bring in scent

A bundle of fresh fir, eucalyptus or cedar in a jar on the console smells like Christmas the moment the door opens. Keep fresh greenery away from radiators.

### 17. Keep it practical

Check that the door opens fully, the walkway is at least 75–90 cm wide, keys still have a home, and nothing blocks a light switch.

## Common Front-Door Slip-Ups
- **A tiny wreath on a big door.** Aim for a third to half the door's width.
- **Indoor lights outside.** Use outdoor-rated ones.
- **Garland on the part of the banister people hold.**
- **Decorating every surface.** The entry needs space for guests.
- **Cords across the step or walkway.**

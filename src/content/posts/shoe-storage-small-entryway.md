Almost every overflowing entryway has the same cause, and it is not a shortage of storage. It is that the whole household's footwear has gravitated to the front door, when only this week's should be there.

So the first job costs nothing.

## Count what actually needs to be there

Gather every pair in the hall and sort into three:

- **This week.** What each person is genuinely wearing — work shoes, trainers, one wet-weather pair.
- **This season.** Worn occasionally. Belongs near the door but not on the floor.
- **Everything else.** Wrong season, rarely worn, formal. Belongs in a wardrobe or under a bed.

The working number for the door is usually **two or three pairs per person**. For a family of four that is eight to twelve pairs, not thirty.

If the third pile is large, the entryway was never the problem. [Small bedroom storage](/post/small-bedroom-storage-ideas/) covers under-bed boxes, which is where most of it should go.

## Then measure, in this order

**Shoe length.** An adult shoe is roughly **28–32 cm**. Measure the largest in the house rather than guessing from an average — a size 46 trainer is what will not fit.

**Available depth.** Measure from the wall to the point where the hallway still has a usable route. In a hall under about 110 cm wide, a 35 cm unit leaves you 75 cm to walk through, which is tight but workable. Below that, you need something shallower.

**Clear height for boots.** **40–45 cm**. Most shelf spacings are 20–25 cm, which is why boots end up on the floor in front of the unit that was bought to hold them.

**The door swing.** Both the front door and any internal door. This rules out more entryway furniture than anything else.

## The options by depth

| Type | Depth needed | Holds | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tilting shoe cabinet | 17–20 cm | Flat shoes on edge, 2–3 per drawer | Narrow halls; the best space-to-capacity ratio |
| Open rack or shelf | 30–35 cm | Anything, including boots on the bottom | Halls with room; wet shoes |
| Bench with storage under | 35–40 cm | Shoes plus a seat | Households that need somewhere to sit |
| Over-door rack | 15 cm | Flat shoes only | Rentals; the back of a cupboard door |
| Basket or tray | 30–40 cm | Wet and muddy shoes | Every hallway, alongside something else |
| Hooks and a tray | Almost none | Hanging boots, wet shoes below | Halls with no floor to spare |

**A tilting cabinet is the one most people should buy.** Storing shoes on edge is what gets the depth down to 17–20 cm, and that is the difference between a unit that fits a narrow hall and one that does not. The compromise: it does not take boots, and it does not suit anything wet.

## Wet shoes need different treatment

This is where closed storage fails. Put a wet shoe in a cabinet and the damp has nowhere to go; it stays in the lining, the leather suffers, and the smell builds.

Wet and muddy footwear wants **open air and a tray**. A boot tray with a raised lip catches the water and can be tipped out. Put a mat in front of it so the worst comes off before the shoes do.

In practice most halls need both: a closed cabinet for the dry rotation and an open tray for whatever came in from the rain.

## If there is genuinely no floor

Some halls have none to give. The route is 80 cm wide and the door opens across most of it.

- **Hooks and a tray.** Coats on hooks, wet shoes on a tray underneath, dry shoes elsewhere in the flat.
- **The back of a cupboard door.** An over-door rack holds flat shoes in 15 cm.
- **Go up.** A shelf at head height for out-of-season shoes in a box — not daily storage, but it clears the floor.
- **Move the storage.** Shoes do not have to live at the door. A cupboard two metres away in the living room works, as long as there is a tray at the door for wet ones.

## Making it stay tidy

**One pair per person on the floor, maximum.** Everything else in the unit. This is the rule that actually holds.

**A tray by the door for today's shoes.** A defined place beats a vague area, and it means the floor pair has somewhere to be.

**Review each season.** Swap sandals for boots rather than keeping both at the door.

## Common mistakes

- **Storing every pair at the door.** Two or three per person.
- **Buying before measuring the largest shoe.** Size 46 is the test.
- **Shelves too close together for boots.** 40–45 cm of clear height.
- **Wet shoes in a closed cabinet.** Damp and smell, both avoidable.
- **Ignoring the door swing.** It decides what fits more than the floor area does.
- **A unit deeper than the hall can give.** Leave 75 cm to walk through.

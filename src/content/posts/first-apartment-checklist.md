The ordinary failure with a first flat is not underspending. It is spending in the wrong order — a sofa and a coffee table arrive before anyone has thought about a shower curtain, and the budget runs out somewhere around the bed.

So buy in the order the day actually uses things: **sleep, wash, eat, sit, store.**

## Night one

A short list, and worth having before the keys rather than after.

- Bed and bedding, including a pillow each
- Towels
- Shower curtain and rings, if the bathroom needs one
- Toilet paper, soap, bin bags
- Kettle or a single pan, one plate, bowl, mug, knife, fork, spoon per person
- One lamp and a bulb — overhead light alone on the first night is bleak
- Cleaning basics, a cloth, and a torch
- Phone charger somewhere you can find it

That is the whole list. Everything else is week two or later.

## Week one

- A second pan and a chopping board
- A drying rack
- Curtains or a blind in the bedroom, even temporary ones
- A bin, and a laundry basket
- A chair to sit on that is not the bed
- Hooks by the door — [how to decorate without nails](/post/how-to-decorate-without-nails/) covers the ones that do not need drilling

## Measure before the big four

Four purchases are worth a tape measure and twenty minutes, because getting them wrong is expensive and hard to undo.

**Anything large, against the route in.** Doorways, stair turns, the lift, the angle at the top of the stairs. A sofa that fits the living room perfectly and not the stairwell is the classic first-flat disaster. Measure the narrowest point of the journey, including diagonals.

**The sofa, against the room.** Walkways want 75–90 cm. One properly sized sofa beats a small sofa plus two chairs — [how to arrange furniture in a small room](/post/how-to-arrange-furniture-in-a-small-room/) has the clearances.

**The bed, against the bedroom.** 60 cm beside a side you get in and out of, 90 cm in front of any wardrobe. [Where to put the bed in a small room](/post/where-to-put-bed-in-small-room/) works through the options.

**Curtains, against the rod rather than the window.** The single most common measuring mistake there is. [How high to hang curtains](/post/how-high-to-hang-curtains/) explains why the rod goes above and beyond the frame.

## What to buy new, and what not to

**New:** the mattress. It is the thing you spend a third of your life on, and second-hand mattresses carry risks that are not worth the saving.

**Second-hand, happily:** solid wood furniture, bookcases, dining chairs, desks, lamps, mirrors, picture frames, baskets, and most kitchen equipment that is not non-stick. These cost a fraction of new and are usually better made.

**Second-hand, carefully:** upholstered furniture. Smells and pests are hard to assess, and fabric holds both. Inspect seams and underneath in good light, or skip it.

## Wait a month on these

The flat will tell you things you cannot know on day one — where the light falls, where you actually sit, which corner collects clutter.

- **Rugs.** You need the furniture in place first. [What size rug do you need](/post/what-size-rug-do-i-need/) explains why measuring the room instead of the furniture goes wrong.
- **Curtains beyond the bedroom.** Temporary ones for privacy are enough.
- **Art and decorative objects.** Easy to add, hard to undo.
- **Storage furniture.** Live with the mess for a few weeks and you will know what shape it is.
- **A second sofa or an armchair.** You may not want it where you thought.

## Three things worth doing in week one

**Photograph everything.** Every wall, every surface, every existing mark, with the date. This is your deposit insurance and it takes ten minutes.

**Find the stopcock, the consumer unit and the meters.** Before you need them at eleven at night.

**Change the bulbs.** 2700K, CRI 90 or above. Rental light fittings are almost universally cool and unflattering, and bulbs are the cheapest improvement available in any flat — see [how to make a dark room brighter](/post/how-to-make-a-dark-room-brighter/).

## Common first-flat mistakes

- **Buying in the wrong order.** Sleep and wash before sit and decorate.
- **Not measuring the route in.** Stairs and doorways, not just the room.
- **A matching furniture set.** It reads as a showroom, and it costs more.
- **A rug before the furniture.** The furniture decides the size.
- **Curtains measured from the frame.** Measure from the rod.
- **Spending the whole budget in month one.** Hold a quarter back.

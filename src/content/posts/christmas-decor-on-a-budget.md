Most Christmas spending goes on the wrong things. Shelves of inexpensive decorations get bought in late November, used once, stored badly, and replaced the following year — which is how a small budget gets spent several times over.

The ranking below is by how much each pound changes the room.

## 1. Lights, bought properly, once

The one thing worth real money. Lights are what change a room after dark, they are used every evening for six weeks, and a decent set lasts years.

What to look for:

- **Warm white**, not cool white. Cold-toned lights are the commonest reason a carefully decorated room still looks wrong — they make greenery grey and metallics cheap.
- **Enough length.** A rough guide is around 100 lights per foot of tree height; running short looks worse than having none.
- **A battery or plug option that suits where it goes**, so you are not running cables across walkways.

Buy once, store well, and this is a cost that does not recur.

## 2. One surface per room, done properly

A small budget spread across five rooms produces five under-decorated rooms. The same money on one surface in each — a mantel, a console, the coffee table — produces something that reads as deliberate.

## 3. The things that cost nothing

December is the one month of the year when the raw materials are lying around outside.

**Foliage.** Cut from your own garden, or gathered where it is permitted to do so — check before taking anything from public land or someone else's hedge. Fir, ivy, eucalyptus, bay. A garland made from cuttings costs the price of the wire.

**Dried orange slices.** Slice thinly, dry in a low oven for a few hours, thread onto string. Costs the price of the oranges, smells like the season, and composts in January.

**Pine cones.** Free. Bake briefly on a low heat on a lined tray to deal with anything living in them, then use them in a bowl or wired into a garland.

**Paper.** Chains, stars, snowflakes. Dismissed as a children's craft, but in good paper and one colour they read as considered rather than cheap.

**Cinnamon sticks and bay leaves**, bundled with twine onto a napkin or a parcel.

What these have in common: they are matte, textural and natural, which is most of what separates expensive-looking decoration from the other kind.

## 4. A tight palette

Free, and it does more than anything you can buy.

Two or three colours across the whole house. Green and cream with brass. Red and green with one dominant. White and silver. The reason inexpensive decorations often look inexpensive is not the price — it is five colours and three metals competing on one shelf.

## 5. Leave space

Also free. Decorating every surface is what makes a room look cluttered rather than festive, and it is what pushes people into buying more in the first place.

## What to stop buying

| Stop buying | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| New baubles every year | Store the ones you have properly |
| Scented candles in quantity | One good one, plus dried orange and cinnamon |
| Themed tableware | Plain plates, seasonal napkins |
| Plastic garlands | Real foliage, or one good faux garland kept for years |
| Cheap cold-white lights | One warm-white set that lasts |
| Novelty decorations | Nothing; they are the definition of used-once |

## The January part that saves next December

Most of what people rebuy each year is replacing what got damaged in storage.

- **Buy boxes in the January sales**, when they are a fraction of December prices.
- **Wind lights around a piece of flat cardboard** rather than coiling them.
- **Egg boxes hold baubles** better than the bags they came in.
- **Label one box per room**, so next year starts with carrying rather than sorting.
- **Compost the foliage and citrus** instead of storing it; it is free again next year.

In a small home, where it all goes is a design decision rather than an afterthought — [small apartment organization ideas](/post/small-apartment-organization-ideas/) covers finding the space.

## Where the budget gets wasted

- **Cold-white lights.** They undo everything else.
- **Spreading it across every room.** One surface each, done well.
- **Five colours and three metals.** Pick two or three.
- **Buying storage in December.** January, at a third of the price.
- **Replacing baubles annually.** Store them properly once.
- **Filling every surface.** Empty space is what makes the rest read.

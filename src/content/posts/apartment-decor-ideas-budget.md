A rented flat looks better because of light, textiles and a few structural fixes, not because of more objects. So the order matters more than the budget. Do the free changes first, spend the first $150 on bulbs, a lamp and textiles, and only then put money into the three big surfaces — the floor, the windows and one wall. Each tier below assumes the one before it is done.

## Tier 1: $0, one afternoon

These cost nothing and often make more difference than the first $100 you spend.

**Turn off the ceiling light.** Use only the lamps you already own in the evening, even if that means moving one from the bedroom. It's a preview of what the bulbs in Tier 2 will do.

**Pull the furniture off the walls.** A sofa pushed flat against the wall with a gap in the middle of the room reads as a waiting room. Bring it 10–20 cm forward, face the seats towards each other, and keep a clear path of 75–90 cm through the room. [How to arrange furniture in a small room](/post/how-to-arrange-furniture-in-a-small-room/) has the walkway widths.

**Put away a third of what's on display.** Then group what's left in threes, on a tray or a stack of books, rather than spreading single objects across every surface.

**Rehang the art at the right height.** Its center belongs about **145 cm (57 in)** from the floor; above a sofa, 20–25 cm above the sofa back. Art hung too high is the most common mistake in a rental. If you're moving a picture to a new hole, check the lease first — many allow small nail holes that you fill at move-out. [How high to hang art](/post/how-high-to-hang-art/) has the spacing for groups.

**Clean the windows.** Free daylight.

## Tier 2: about $150, light and textiles

| Item | Typical price | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2700K LED bulbs, about 8 | $20–$35 | Cool white is why rentals feel institutional |
| One floor lamp | $40–$80 | A second light source at a different height |
| Two cushion covers | $15–$40 | Covers, not cushions; keep the inserts |
| A throw | $25–$60 | The texture you touch most |
| Two easy plants | $15–$30 | Pothos, snake plant or ZZ plant |
| **A $150 version** | **About $145** | Mid-range picks from each line |

These are typical ranges at mainstream stores and vary by region; check current prices before you plan.

**Bulbs first.** **2700K warm white** in every fitting you use in the evening. If you do one thing on this list, do this one, and you'll see the difference the same night.

**Then a second light source.** Two or three lamps at different heights in a room — a floor lamp by the sofa, a table lamp, something low on a shelf — make a rental feel like a home. A second-hand lamp base with a new linen shade looks better than the reverse.

**Textiles do the decorating.** Two cushion covers in one color from the room, a throw over the sofa arm, and a duvet cover with texture rather than pattern. They're cheap, you touch them every day, and they move with you. Plants are the cheapest thing that makes a room look lived in, and pothos and spider plants grow new plants from cuttings in a glass of water.

## Tier 3: about $250 more, the big surfaces

| Item | Typical price | What to buy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A rug that fits | $100–$250 | 160 × 230 cm in a small flat; flatweave or low pile |
| Floor-length curtains, two panels | $40–$100 | Plain linen-look in a warm neutral |
| One large print and frame | $40–$100 | One big piece instead of several small ones |
| **A $250 version** | **About $250** | A $150 rug, $50 curtains and a $50 print |

**The rug.** The most common budget mistake is a rug that's too small, which makes a room look smaller and cheaper. Work from the seating: the front legs of every seat sit on it, with a margin of bare floor around the edge. A large cheap rug beats a small expensive one, so spend on size, not fiber. [What size rug do I need](/post/what-size-rug-do-i-need/) has the sizes for every room.

**The curtains.** Hang them **10–15 cm above the frame** — or just under the ceiling if the gap is less than about 30 cm — and **15–25 cm past each side**, so the fabric stacks off the glass. Buy panels that add up to 2–2.5 times the window width and let them reach the floor. Length matters more than fabric. In a rental, use the existing brackets if they're close, or ask before moving them; [how high to hang curtains](/post/how-high-to-hang-curtains/) has the measurements.

**One large piece of art.** A single large print makes a room look bigger and more considered than six small frames, and usually costs less. Museum-shop downloads printed locally, a large fabric piece or your own photograph printed big all work.

## Where to spend more, and where to save

| Spend on | Save on |
| --- | --- |
| A good mattress | Side tables and shelving — second-hand or flat-pack |
| Bedding and towels | Cushion inserts and covers |
| A desk chair, if you work from home | Prints rather than originals |
| A large rug | Plant pots |
| One good lamp | Decorative objects and anything trend-driven |

The pattern: pay for what you use every day and take to the next flat.

## The cautious tier: peel-and-stick

Removable wallpaper, floor tiles in a small bathroom, or film on a dated splashback can change a room — and can lift paint when they come off, especially on matt emulsion in a warm room. Test on a hidden patch, leave it a week, then peel slowly and look at what came with it. Get written agreement before covering anything large. The details for kitchens and bathrooms are in [rental kitchen makeover](/post/rental-kitchen-makeover/) and [rental bathroom makeover](/post/rental-bathroom-makeover/).

## Make a move-out kit as you go

Keep one labeled box with everything you swap out: the original bulbs, cabinet knobs, shower head and curtain rods. Add a small tub of wall filler, a putty knife and, if the landlord can tell you the color, a little touch-up paint. Moving out then takes an hour rather than a weekend, and the deposit conversation is a short one.

## Where budget rentals go wrong

- **Buying decor before fixing the light.** Light changes everything else.
- **A tiny rug.** It makes the room look smaller. Bigger and cheaper wins.
- **Curtains at the frame and above the floor.** High, wide and long.
- **Art hung too high.** Center it at about 145 cm.
- **Peel-and-stick without a test patch.** It can take the paint with it.
- **Throwing away the original fittings.** You'll need them at move-out.

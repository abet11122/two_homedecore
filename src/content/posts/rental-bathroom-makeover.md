A rented bathroom is the room with the most fixed surfaces and the least permission to change them. It is also the room where deposits are most often lost, because steam attacks every adhesive you might be tempted to use.

So: photograph every surface — including grout, sealant and the inside of the cabinet — before touching anything. Then work from the outside in, starting with the things that are not attached to the building.

## 1. The bulb

The same first move as everywhere else, and it costs almost nothing. **2700–3000K, CRI 90 or above.** Rented bathrooms almost universally have a cool bulb in a single ceiling fitting, which turns white tile grey and makes everyone look ill.

Position matters more than brightness, which in a bathroom means light at the mirror rather than overhead — [bathroom lighting ideas](/post/bathroom-lighting-ideas/) covers what is possible without an electrician, and what definitely is not.

## 2. The shower curtain, hung high

The single biggest visual change available in a rented bathroom, and it is entirely reversible.

Buy an **extra-long curtain** and hang it close to the ceiling rather than at the standard height, on a longer tension rod. The eye reads one tall line instead of a short one, the room's proportions change, and a tired shower enclosure disappears behind fabric.

It is the same trick as [hanging curtains high and wide](/post/how-high-to-hang-curtains/) in a living room, and it works for the same reason.

## 3. Textiles

A bathroom has very few soft surfaces, so the ones it has do a disproportionate amount of work.

- **One set of good towels in a single colour.** Mismatched towels in four colours are most of what makes a bathroom look chaotic.
- **A bath mat with some weight.** The thin free one that came with nothing is a giveaway.
- **A fabric shower curtain** rather than plastic, with a cheap plastic liner behind it.

## 4. Freestanding storage

Rented bathrooms are nearly always short of it, which is why every surface fills up.

A slim shelf unit, an over-toilet freestanding frame, a trolley in a gap, stacking boxes inside the vanity. All of it leaves with you, which makes it the best money in the room. [Small bathroom organization ideas](/post/small-bathroom-organization-ideas/) covers measuring the zones, and [over-toilet storage](/post/bathroom-over-toilet-storage-ideas/) has the heights for the one wall most people waste.

## 5. Clear the surfaces

Free, and it changes the room more than most purchases. Work out what genuinely needs to live out — usually far less than currently does — and put the rest in the storage from the previous step.

## 6. The cautious tier

This is where deposits go, so treat it as a different category rather than more of the same.

**Steam is the problem.** A bathroom is warmer and wetter than a kitchen, and adhesive bonds behave accordingly: they grip harder over time, and water gets behind any edge that lifts.

If you are considering peel-and-stick tile, vinyl flooring or removable wallpaper:

1. Get the landlord's agreement **in writing**.
2. Test a piece somewhere hidden — inside a cabinet door, behind the pedestal.
3. Leave it **at least a week**, then peel it slowly and look at what came away.
4. Keep everything well clear of the shower, the bath and the splash zone around the basin.

The same cautions, with more detail on removal technique, are in [renter-friendly backsplash ideas](/post/renter-friendly-backsplash-ideas/).

**Painting** needs permission and the right product — a bathroom-formulated paint, never matt emulsion. [Small bathroom paint colors](/post/small-bathroom-paint-colors/) covers the finish and the reflectance that actually decides how bright the room reads.

## What to spend on

| Worth it | Think twice |
| --- | --- |
| A good bulb | Adhesive tile anywhere near water |
| A long shower curtain and rod | Anything needing new holes |
| Towels and a bath mat | Replacing the landlord's fittings |
| Freestanding storage | Permanent flooring |
| A large mirror or a plant | Re-grouting that was not your job |

The pattern, as in [rental kitchen makeover](/post/rental-kitchen-makeover/): spend on things that move house with you.

## One thing worth raising with the landlord

If the extractor fan is weak or missing, that is their problem and it is worth reporting in writing. Poor ventilation causes mould, mould causes disputes at the end of a tenancy, and a documented report protects you. It also does more for the room than anything in this article.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Adhesive anything in the splash zone.** Steam and water win.
- **No test patch.** A week minimum, somewhere hidden.
- **Matt emulsion.** It fails in a bathroom.
- **Mismatched towels.** One colour, and the room settles.
- **A short shower curtain at standard height.** The cheapest fix in the room, missed.
- **Not photographing the room on day one.** That record is your deposit.

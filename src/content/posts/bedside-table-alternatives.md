Before looking at alternatives, it helps to be clear about what a nightstand actually does. There are three jobs: hold a **light**, hold a **drink**, and hold the **small things you reach for in the dark** — phone, book, glasses, inhaler.

Most of the options below do two of the three. Once you know which job matters most to you, the choice gets simple — and if the lamp can go on the wall, the surface you need shrinks dramatically.

One measurement governs all of them: the usable height is **roughly level with the top of the mattress**, give or take 5 cm. Measure yours with the bedding on.

## Wall-mounted, no floor needed

**A floating shelf.** The workhorse. 20–25 cm deep is enough for a lamp, a glass and a book, and it takes no floor at all. Fit it at mattress height. Because it is open underneath, the room reads as less full than it would with a cabinet — the point made in [how to make a bedroom look bigger](/post/how-to-make-a-bedroom-look-bigger/).

**A floating drawer.** The same idea with a closed drawer underneath, for people who want things out of sight. Heavier, so the fixing matters more.

**A wall sconce plus a tiny ledge.** The best answer for a genuinely tight gap. Moving the lamp onto the wall frees the whole surface, and a 12–15 cm ledge then handles the glass and the phone. A swing-arm sconce doubles as a reading light.

**A pegboard.** A small pegboard beside the bed takes a shelf, a hook for headphones and a cup holder, and can be rearranged. Suits a child's or a teenager's room — see [teenage boy bedroom ideas](/post/teenage-boy-bedroom-ideas/).

**On fixings:** anything carrying a lamp and a full glass of water needs a proper anchor rated above the load, not a picture pin. Identify the wall first — the [floating shelf checklist](/post/diy-floating-shelves/) goes through the types and the right fixing for each.

## Freestanding, under 40 cm

**A stool.** A plain wooden or woven stool, 30–40 cm across. Cheap, moves anywhere, doubles as a seat, and looks deliberate rather than makeshift.

**A narrow plant stand.** Often 25–30 cm square with a lower shelf, which gives you two levels in a very small footprint.

**A C-shaped side table.** The base slides under the bed and the top cantilevers over it, so the usable surface sits above the mattress without needing floor beside it. Excellent where the gap is almost nothing.

**A chest of drawers turned end-on.** If you have a small chest and a narrow gap, standing it with its short side facing out sometimes fits where a nightstand will not, and buys you real drawer space.

**A stack of books.** Genuinely works, costs nothing, and holds a lamp and a glass. Keep the stack under about 40 cm or it gets unstable.

## Attached to the bed

**A hanging caddy.** Fabric pockets that hook over the bed frame or slide between mattress and base. Holds a phone, a book and a charger. No floor, no wall, no fixings — the obvious choice in a rental where you cannot drill and have no space. Not a surface for a drink.

**A clamp-on tray.** Clamps to the bed frame and swings a small tray over the mattress. Good for a bunk or a loft bed, where there is no adjacent surface at all. [Small boys bedroom ideas](/post/small-boys-bedroom-ideas/) covers bunk arrangements where this is often the only option.

## Picking one

| Gap beside the bed | Best options |
| --- | --- |
| None at all | Wall sconce plus ledge, hanging caddy, clamp-on tray |
| 20–30 cm | Floating shelf, narrow plant stand, C-shaped table |
| 30–40 cm | Stool, book stack, chest turned end-on |
| 40 cm or more | A conventional nightstand will fit — measure before assuming otherwise |

If the bed is in a corner and only one side is accessible, you only need one of these. That is not a compromise; it is just what the room is. [Where to put the bed in a small room](/post/where-to-put-bed-in-small-room/) covers how that decision gets made.

## What to avoid

- **A surface well below mattress height.** Reaching down for a glass in the dark goes badly.
- **A shelf on the wrong fixing.** A lamp plus a full glass is a real load.
- **Anything rigid directly over a pillow.** Beside the bed, not above it.
- **Something too small for a glass.** If a drink will not fit, the surface is decorative.
- **Matching pairs you cannot fit.** Two different surfaces beat one shared one.

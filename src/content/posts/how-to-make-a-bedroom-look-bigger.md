A lot of advice on this subject is repeated so often that nobody checks whether it works. Painting everything white, buying small furniture, hanging mirrors everywhere — some of it helps a little, some does nothing, and one or two make things worse. Here is the separation.

## What genuinely works

### Show the floor

Visible floor is the single strongest cue the eye uses to judge a room's size. Anything that reveals more of it helps, and anything that covers it hurts.

In practice: furniture on **visible legs** rather than solid bases or skirts. A bed frame with 15 cm of clear floor underneath reads as considerably more open than a divan sitting flat, even though they occupy identical floor area. The same goes for a chest of drawers on legs and a bedside table with an open shelf.

The cost is under-bed storage, which in a small bedroom is often too valuable to trade away. If you need it, keep the storage bed and find the gain elsewhere.

### Use fewer, larger pieces

The instinct is to buy small furniture for a small room. It backfires: five small items read as clutter, one correctly sized item reads as calm.

One decent wardrobe beats a small wardrobe plus a rail plus two storage boxes. One large picture beats six small frames. The number of objects matters more than the size of each.

### Hang the curtains high and wide

Rod **10–15 cm above the frame**, extended **15–25 cm past it on each side**, curtains that **reach the floor**. The eye reads an uninterrupted vertical from ceiling to floor and the wall gains apparent height.

This is free if you are moving an existing rod, and it is one of the biggest changes available. [How high to hang curtains](/post/how-high-to-hang-curtains/) has the measurements.

### Reduce the contrast between surfaces

Every strong edge is something for the eye to measure. Walls, ceiling, woodwork and curtains that sit close in tone remove those edges and the room's boundaries blur.

Note that this does **not** mean white. A bedroom painted a single deep colour — walls, woodwork and often the ceiling — routinely looks larger than a white room with brilliant white trim and a dark floor, because the dark room has fewer internal edges. [Cozy small bedroom ideas](/post/cozy-small-bedroom-ideas/) leans into that version.

### Clear the surfaces

Not a design trick, just the most reliable one. Pick the surfaces in the room and halve what is on them.

### Light it at more than one height

A single ceiling bulb flattens a room and pushes every shadow into the corners, which makes those corners read as walls. A bedside lamp and something indirect give the room depth. [How to make a dark room brighter](/post/how-to-make-a-dark-room-brighter/) covers bulb choice.

## What does less than people claim

**Painting everything white.** On its own, very little. A white room with dark skirting, a dark floor and a full set of furniture has just as many edges as it did before. The contrast is what matters, not the colour.

**Lots of small mirrors.** One large mirror placed to catch a window works. A collection of small ones adds objects, and objects are what you are trying to reduce. A leaning full-length mirror is usually the better buy.

**Small-scale furniture.** Covered above — it usually makes things worse.

**Pushing everything against the walls.** It feels like it should free the middle. It produces a room that looks like a waiting area, with a void in the centre and furniture lined up around the edge.

**Vertical stripes.** They add height on paper. In a bedroom they mostly add pattern, which adds visual noise.

**A tiny rug.** A rug too small for the bed chops the floor into pieces and makes the room read smaller. Either size it properly or leave the floor bare — [what size rug do you need](/post/what-size-rug-do-i-need/) has the bedroom measurements.

## If you only do three things

1. Move the curtain rod up and out, and get the curtains to the floor.
2. Take half of everything off the surfaces.
3. Add a second light source at a different height.

None costs much, all three are reversible, and together they will do more than repainting the room.

## Where people go wrong

- **White paint as the whole strategy.** Reduce contrast, not just colour.
- **Buying small furniture.** Fewer and larger.
- **A skirted bed in a room with no storage problem.** Show the floor.
- **Short curtains.** They shorten the wall.
- **Several small mirrors.** One big one, placed deliberately.
- **Everything against the walls.** Leave the arrangement some asymmetry.

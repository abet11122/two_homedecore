Three things kill balcony plants, and none of them is a lack of gardening skill: the weight limit nobody checked, pots too small for the wind, and water going somewhere it should not. Sort those and the plant list is the easy part.

## Weight comes first

Saturated compost weighs roughly **1.5 to 2 kg per litre**. A 40-litre container — not a large one — can therefore weigh **60 kg or more** after watering, before you add the pot and the plant.

Six of those is the weight of several people standing in one spot.

Balcony load limits vary by building, age and construction, and there is no figure that applies to all of them. **Ask the building management or check the lease** before planning anything substantial. Where you are unsure, keep heavy containers near the wall or over the structural edge rather than massed in the middle of the span, and spread the weight rather than concentrating it.

Lighter options, if weight is a genuine constraint:

- **Fibreglass, resin or plastic pots** instead of terracotta or concrete.
- **Peat-free compost blended with perlite**, which reduces weight considerably.
- **Railing planters** rather than floor-standing ones, where the railing is rated for it and the building allows it.
- **Fewer, better-chosen containers.**

## Then work out which way it faces

This decides the plant list more than anything else, and it takes thirty seconds with a phone compass.

- **South-facing** — sun most of the day, and hot. Mediterranean herbs, tomatoes, chillies, pelargoniums. Everything will need more water than you expect.
- **North-facing** — shade. Ferns, hostas, heuchera, ivy. A genuinely good shade balcony beats a struggling sun one.
- **East** — morning sun. Kind conditions for most things.
- **West** — afternoon sun and usually the most wind. Tough, drought-tolerant planting.

Then watch it for a week. Buildings cast shadows that a compass does not know about, and the balcony above yours may be taking half your light and all of your rain.

## Big pots are easier than small ones

The instinct on a small balcony is lots of small pots. It is the most common reason balcony gardens fail.

A small pot in wind is a tiny amount of compost with a large exposed surface. On a hot, breezy day it can **dry out completely within a day**. Small pots also blow over, which is both a loss and a hazard several floors up.

Fewer, larger containers hold moisture for longer, stay put, and are much more forgiving of a weekend away. If you want variety, plant several things into one large container rather than buying several small ones.

Minimum useful sizes, roughly: **20 litres** for herbs and salad, **30–40 litres** for a tomato or a small shrub, **50 litres and up** for anything intended as screening.

## Plan where the water goes

The classic balcony complaint is water running onto the flat below — and it is almost always avoidable.

- **A saucer or tray under every pot.** Non-negotiable.
- **Water slowly**, in two passes, rather than emptying a can in one go.
- **Never let a pot drain through the railing gaps.**
- **Self-watering containers** with a reservoir, which are worth the extra cost on a balcony for the plants as much as the neighbours.
- **Check the balcony's own drainage** is not blocked by pots or screening.

## A starting list that works

**Herbs** — rosemary, thyme, oregano and sage in sun; mint in its own pot, always, because it takes over anything it shares with.

**Salad leaves** — fast, cheap, and happy in a trough. Cut and come again.

**Tomatoes and chillies** — reliable in a sunny spot in a 30-litre-plus pot. Needs consistent watering or the fruit splits.

**Tall grasses** — the best balcony screening plant: light, moves in the wind rather than fighting it, and looks good for most of the year. See [small balcony privacy ideas](/post/small-balcony-privacy-ideas/) for using them that way.

**Ferns and hostas** — for a shaded balcony, and better than struggling with sun-lovers in the wrong place.

**Pelargoniums** — drought-tolerant, long-flowering, and forgiving of the week you forget.

## Through the year

**Spring** — repot, top up compost, check fixings on anything attached.

**Summer** — water early or late rather than at midday; expect daily watering in a heatwave.

**Autumn** — cut back, move tender plants against the wall, and tie in anything that catches wind. The dried stems from tall grasses carry straight into [the autumn mantel](/post/autumn-mantel-styling/).

**Winter** — group pots together against the wall for shelter, raise them on feet so they drain, and accept that some things will not make it.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Not asking about the weight limit.** Wet compost is much heavier than it looks.
- **Lots of small pots.** They dry out in a day and blow over.
- **Buying plants before checking the aspect.** The compass first.
- **No saucers.** The neighbour below will mention it.
- **Mint planted with anything else.** It wins.
- **Blocking the balcony drainage.** Water needs a route out.

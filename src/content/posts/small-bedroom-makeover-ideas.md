A budget small bedroom makeover works best in a fixed order: clear and rearrange (free), paint (cheap and high-impact), change textiles and lighting, then buy furniture only where it solves a real problem. Most of the change happens in the first three steps, and they fit into a weekend.

This plan is for a typical small bedroom of about 3 × 3 m (10 × 10 ft). Each step includes what it costs, so you can stop when the budget does.

## Friday Evening: Clear and Plan (Free)

### Step 1: Empty the surfaces

Take everything off the dresser, nightstands, chair and floor. Put back only what you use daily or love. Anything that "lives" on the chair needs a real home — usually a hook or a hamper.

### Step 2: Try a new layout

Most small bedrooms have only one or two sensible bed positions. Try them before buying anything:

- Put the bed on the wall you see first when you walk in.
- Keep about 60 cm on at least one side, and 75–90 cm in front of the wardrobe and drawers.
- In a narrow room, try the long side of the bed against the wall — it frees a wide strip of floor.
- Don't block radiators or the window.

Measure, sketch, and move the bed if needed. It's free and often makes the biggest difference.

### Step 3: Write a shopping list

Decide what the room actually lacks: light, storage, softness, or color. Buy for those problems only.

## Saturday: Paint ($50–$120)

### Step 4: Work out how much paint you need

**Example:** a 3 × 3 m room with a 2.4 m ceiling has 4 walls × 3 m × 2.4 m = 28.8 m². Subtract a door (about 1.6 m²) and a window (about 1.5 m²): roughly 25.7 m². At 10–12 m² per liter per coat, two coats need about 4.5–5 liters. Buy one 5-liter tin, or a little over a US gallon, and a sample pot first.

### Step 5: Choose the color and finish

- **Light warm neutrals** — warm white, greige, pale sage — bounce light around a small room.
- **Deeper colors** — clay, deep green, navy — make a small room feel cozy rather than small, especially with warm lamps.
- **Finish:** matte or eggshell on walls. Gloss shows every lump.
- **Color drenching:** painting walls, skirting and door in one color makes edges disappear and can make a small room feel larger.

### Step 6: Prep and paint

Fill holes, sand, and wipe down the walls. Tape the edges. Cut in around the edges with a brush, then roll the walls. Wait the drying time on the tin before the second coat. Open a window and leave the room to air overnight.

## Sunday: Textiles and Light ($100–$300)

### Step 7: Update the bedding

A new duvet cover and sheets change the room more than anything except paint. Choose:

- **Plain or subtle texture** — linen, waffle, washed cotton — for a calm, expensive look.
- **One throw** folded across the bottom third of the bed.
- **Two decorative cushions**, not ten.

Coordinate with the wall color: a tone lighter or darker, or one accent color repeated twice in the room.

### Step 8: Hang curtains high and wide

Mount the rod 10–15 cm above the window frame and extend it 15–25 cm past each side. Curtains should just touch the floor. The window looks bigger and the ceiling higher. Ready-made curtains in standard lengths (for example, 228 cm or 90 in) are inexpensive.

### Step 9: Fix the lighting

Replace bright cool bulbs with warm white 2700K. Add a bedside lamp or a plug-in wall sconce each side. Turn off the overhead light in the evening. This alone changes the mood of the room.

### Step 10: Add a rug underfoot

A runner on the side you get up from, or a 120 × 170 cm rug under the lower part of the bed, adds softness and warmth. Flatweave and low-pile rugs are cheaper and easier to clean.

## Next Few Weeks: Storage and Furniture ($50–$500)

### Step 11: Use the wall above the bed

A picture ledge or one large print centered above the headboard gives the room a focal point. Hang art so its bottom edge is 20–30 cm above the headboard.

### Step 12: Add storage where clutter lands

- Hooks behind the door for robes and tomorrow's outfit.
- Under-bed drawers for bedding and off-season clothes (measure the gap first).
- A floating shelf instead of a bedside table in a tight room.

See [small bedroom storage](/post/small-bedroom-storage-ideas/) for clearances and under-bed measurements.

### Step 13: Swap one bulky piece

If a big piece of furniture blocks the room, replace it with something slimmer or with legs — legs show more floor and make a room feel bigger. A tall, narrow chest of drawers stores as much as a wide low one in half the floor space.

### Step 14: Update hardware

New knobs or handles on a dresser or wardrobe cost a few dollars each and make old furniture look intentional. Measure the hole spacing on existing handles before buying.

### Step 15: Add a mirror

A large mirror opposite or beside the window doubles the daylight. Lean a full-length mirror against a wall (and secure it with an anti-tip strap), or mount one on the back of the door.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying furniture first.** Do the free and cheap steps first.
- **Too many small decorative items.** One big print beats ten tiny frames.
- **Curtain rods on the window frame.** Go higher and wider.
- **Skipping wall prep.** Paint shows every hole you didn't fill.
- **Cool-white bulbs.** They undo all the warmth of new bedding.

## What It Costs

| Stage | What's included | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Declutter, new layout | $0 |
| Paint | 5 L paint, roller, tape, filler | $50–$120 |
| Textiles and light | Bedding, curtains, bulbs, one lamp | $150–$300 |
| Storage and finishing | Hooks, under-bed drawers, a shelf, handles, a mirror | $100–$300 |

Prices vary by region and store.

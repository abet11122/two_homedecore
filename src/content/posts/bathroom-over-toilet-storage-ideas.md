Over-toilet storage works when it's shallow, mounted high enough to lift the cistern lid, and holds only light items. Measure the clear wall width, the height above the cistern, and your head clearance when standing — then choose between open shelves, a closed cabinet or a freestanding frame based on what you need to store.

The wall above a toilet is often the biggest unused surface in a small bathroom. It's also where bad measurements show up fastest: a shelf that blocks the lid, a cabinet you bump every morning, a frame that makes cleaning around the base impossible.

## Step 1: Take Five Measurements

1. **Clear wall width** — from the nearest obstruction on one side (a vanity, towel rail, window frame, corner) to the other. Toilets typically need about 38 cm (15 in) from their centerline to any side obstruction, so the usable wall is often 70–90 cm wide.
2. **Height above the cistern** — from the top of the cistern lid to the ceiling.
3. **Cistern depth** — from the wall to the front of the cistern lid. Your storage shouldn't stick out much further than this.
4. **Lid clearance** — lift the cistern lid straight up and note how much room you need to get it off.
5. **Head height** — sit on the toilet, stand up, and mark on the wall where your head and shoulders pass. Do this for the tallest person in the house.

Write these down. They decide everything that follows.

## Step 2: Choose the Type That Solves Your Problem

| Type | Best for | Watch out for | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open floating shelves (2–3) | Towels, spare rolls, a plant; easy reach | Dust, steam, visual clutter | $25–$70 |
| Closed wall cabinet | Hiding mixed packaging; keeping towels splash-free | Door swing, weight on the wall | $60–$200 |
| Freestanding frame | Rentals; walls you can't drill | Legs around the toilet base, anti-tip strap | $40–$120 |
| Single ledge or picture shelf | Very narrow walls; decor only | Holds very little | $15–$30 |

**Open shelves** make spare rolls and folded hand towels easy to grab. Choose a shelf with a lip, or put loose items in a basket, so nothing slides off. Two shelves about 30 cm apart are usually more useful than three crammed together.

**A closed cabinet** hides visual clutter and keeps clean towels away from splashes. Check where the doors swing — a 40 cm wide door needs 40 cm of clear space in front when open. Mirrored doors can make a small bathroom feel larger.

**A freestanding frame** straddles the toilet and stands on the floor. Measure around the toilet base, the water supply pipe and the skirting board, and make sure the legs don't sit where you'll want to mop. "Freestanding" doesn't mean it can't tip: use the anti-tip bracket that comes with it.

## Step 3: Get the Height and Depth Right

- **Depth:** 15–23 cm (6–9 in). In a narrow bathroom, stay at the low end.
- **Bottom of the lowest shelf or cabinet:** about 60–75 cm above the cistern lid, or higher if your head-height mark from Step 1 is above that.
- **Spacing between shelves:** 25–35 cm, depending on what you'll store. Rolled hand towels need about 20 cm; a basket of spare rolls needs 25–30 cm.
- **Width:** leave at least 5 cm on each side so the unit doesn't look jammed in.

**Example:** the wall is 75 cm wide and the cistern is 18 cm deep. A 60 cm wide, 18 cm deep cabinet leaves 7.5 cm each side and sits flush with the cistern front. Mounted 65 cm above the lid, it clears a 1.8 m person's head when standing. That's a good fit. A 30 cm deep cabinet on the same wall would stick out 12 cm past the cistern and likely get bumped.

## Step 4: Install It Safely

1. **Find out what the wall is made of.** Tap it — drywall sounds hollow, masonry and tile sound solid. Each needs different fixings.
2. **Check for pipes and wiring.** The supply pipe usually runs up from the floor behind or beside the toilet, and there may be pipes inside the wall above. Use a stud and pipe detector before you drill.
3. **Use the right fixings.** In drywall, screw into studs (usually 40 cm or 16 in apart), or use heavy-duty hollow-wall anchors rated for the load. In tile, use a tile drill bit and drill slowly without hammer action.
4. **Level it.** Use a spirit level and mark the holes through the bracket.
5. **Load test.** Fill it with what you plan to store and leave it for a day before adding anything else.

If the wall is old plaster, uneven, or you're not sure what's behind it, get help. A cabinet full of towels falling onto a ceramic toilet is an expensive mistake.

## Step 5: Decide What Belongs Overhead

Good candidates:

- Spare toilet rolls (in a basket or lidded box)
- Two or three folded or rolled hand towels
- Cotton pads or buds in a lidded jar
- A small plant that tolerates low light and humidity, like a pothos or snake plant

Keep these elsewhere:

- Glass bottles, perfume and anything heavy or breakable
- Medicines — many need a cool, dry place, and some should be locked away from children
- Cleaning products — store them securely and out of reach
- Anything you use every morning, if the shelf is hard to reach

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Measuring the toilet but not your head.** The shelf fits the wall but not the person using it.
- **Blocking the cistern lid.** One day you'll need to fix the flush mechanism.
- **Skipping the anti-tip strap on a frame.** It's in the box for a reason.
- **Overloading open shelves.** More than a few items reads as clutter in a small room.
- **Forgetting the cleaning.** Make sure you can still reach behind and around the toilet base with a cloth.

## Check It After a Week

Lift the cistern lid, flush, clean around the base, and take an item off the top shelf. If any of those is awkward, adjust now. Every few months, check that fixings are still tight and that steam hasn't warped the shelves or loosened adhesive.
